Product 1 · Ignition Forecast

Where and when the next fires will start

Wintel is a daily, self-verifying forecast of wildfire ignition. Every morning it ranks every district in a region by the calibrated probability of a new fire tomorrow, locates it within 1 km, and checks yesterday's forecast against the fires that actually appeared. It runs on free global data, so any region on Earth can be switched on within days.

How It Works

Every morning, unattended

A scheduled job on free cloud infrastructure runs the whole chain before the operations briefing. Nothing on a laptop, nothing to install.

  1. Ingest

    Overnight satellite fire detections (NASA FIRMS, VIIRS) and today's weather and forecast (ERA5 and open-meteo) for every district. Roads, power lines, population and fuel from OpenStreetMap, WorldPop and national land surveys.

  2. Forecast

    85 variables per district-day feed a gradient-boosted model, calibrated so the output is a real probability. A second model distributes it within the district to 1 km cells. Separate models cover two and three days out.

  3. Verify

    The previous days' forecasts are scored against the fires that actually appeared: the Wintel top-10, the historical top-10, the fire-weather-index top-10 and the 1 km zones. Nothing is edited afterwards.

  4. Publish

    Map, ranking, probabilities and verification go to the dashboard, with CSV and GeoJSON downloads for your own systems. The daily archive is the record you audit us against.

What it is, and what it is not

  • Occurrence, not spread. Wintel says where and when a new fire is likely to start. It does not simulate how a fire will grow once it has started; your spread simulator and detection network keep doing that job, and Wintel feeds the decision made before them.
  • A ranking first, a probability second. The index orders districts and cells for pre-positioning. The probability column is calibrated on eleven out-of-sample seasons so that “15%” means fifteen such days in a hundred had a new fire. At two and three days the probability is approximate and the dashboard says so.
  • Trained on what we can see. Where no official register is available the label is a satellite-detected new fire, which misses small fires put out before the overpass. With your ignition register the label changes and nothing else does; that is the first thing a pilot adds, and it improved the pilot region's result against the official register to 42% vs 38%.
  • Honest on bad days. On a day when fifty fires start across the whole region, prioritising area helps little, and the verification page shows it. That page is the trust mechanism, not a black box.
The Missing Layer

Nobody else sells this

Detection vendors (cameras, satellites, sensors) act after ignition. Spread and fire-weather systems say when, not where. Wintel is the layer in front of both: a calibrated, daily-verified forecast of where and when, and it plugs into what you already run.

Then: where crews should be based

  • Five layers on one grid: occurrence, escape, assets at risk, arrival time, and the risk no base can reach in 30 minutes
  • Your bases, your inventory: the prototype uses assumed bases and plantation values; a customer's own replace them
