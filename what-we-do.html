Our Services

What We Do

Two products and four published capabilities. The products are a daily forecast of where and when the next fires will start, and the deployment layer that turns it into where crews should be based. Around them sit the methods we bring to pilots and research partnerships: sub-hourly fire potential, escalation drivers, climate projections, and what a forecast is worth.

The Product

Daily Ignition Forecast: Where and When

Every morning, for every district in a region, the calibrated probability of a new fire tomorrow, located within 1 km and checked against the fires that actually appeared. Live over 86 districts of southern Chile since September 2026; built on free global data, so any region can be switched on within days.

  • 41% vs 35% of next-day fires caught by the ten priority districts, against historical frequency; 11 of 11 seasons won
  • Within 1 km, using roads, power lines, population and fuel: 65% more fires caught than history-based zones of the same area
  • Verified every day against the fires that appeared; a bad day is shown like a good one
  • Any region: the same engine beats the best fire-weather-index model in 68 of 68 held-out seasons across seven regions on four continents

Product 2 · Risk & Deployment

Where Crews Should Be Based Tomorrow

The ignition forecast weighted by the probability that a fire escapes and by the plantation at stake around it, with arrival time by road from each crew base. Five layers on one 1 km grid, with bases you can move on the map and export; your own bases, inventory and fire register replace the assumptions.

  • 2× the escaped fires caught in the same 1,000 km²: 20% against 11% for occurrence alone, in 11 of 11 seasons
  • Escape from access: fires starting 27 minutes or more from a settlement escape ten times as often as those within two
  • Out-of-reach sectors flagged for any base layout at a 20, 30 or 45 minute threshold
  • Verified against the official fire register, by layer, day by day and for the season

Forecasting

Real-Time Fire Potential Forecasting

Operational fire danger is typically issued once a day, while the fire environment changes hour to hour. Our machine learning models read live automatic weather station feeds and output fire potential at sub-hourly intervals, closing that gap.

  • Continuous fire potential updated sub-hourly, not once a day
  • Transfer learning across regions, so a model trained where records are rich can be carried to where they are not
  • Skill assessed against operational practice, benchmarked on fire-behaviour-index baselines rather than in isolation
  • Multi-channel delivery via API, dashboard, SMS, or email alerts

Escalation

What Makes a Fire Escalate

We model the probability that a fire crosses successive size thresholds given ignition, and quantify which part of the fire-environment triangle — fuels, topography, weather — carries the signal at each scale. Across 116,771 New Zealand records, that weighting shifts systematically with fire size.

  • Escalation probability at 0.1, 1, 10, and 100 ha, spanning three orders of magnitude
  • Landscape constrains early growth — fuels and topography dominate up to 10 ha, while atmospheric forcing becomes comparable at 100 ha
  • Scale-dependent terrain control, shifting from slope at small sizes to elevation at the largest threshold
  • Auditable importance via feature-dropout testing, so every driver ranking can be checked rather than trusted

Projections

Fire Growth Under Climate Change

We carry a trained fire model onto gridded climate projections using delta-ALE spatial transfer learning. Each 5 km cell is anchored to its own historical baseline, so the projected change reflects local conditions rather than a national average.

  • National 5 km projections across CMIP6 scenarios, from SSP1-2.6 to SSP5-8.5
  • Driver attribution showing which climate variable produces the projected change, and where
  • Seasonal shift quantified — spring fire-growth probability rising toward present-day summer levels
  • Stated limits — bootstrap confidence intervals and an applicability check reported alongside every projection

Decision Value

Forecast Skill, Priced

A more skilful forecast only matters if acting on it beats acting without it. We evaluate forecasts in a cost–loss framework and report potential economic value, so you can see at which warning threshold the forecast pays for the organisation using it.

  • Cost–loss analysis tied to your own pre-positioning and suppression costs
  • Potential economic value mapped across the full range of warning thresholds, not just one operating point
  • Current practice as the baseline, so the comparison is against what you do today
  • An adoption decision, not just a metric — where the forecast earns its place in the workflow
